I'll use the following notation conventions:

  • $$ \hat{H} $$ is the total Hamiltonian, often split as $$ \hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 + \hat{V}(t) $$ where $$ \hat{H}_0 $$ is the unperturbed (free) part and $$ \hat{V}(t) $$ is the interaction or perturbation (possibly time-dependent).
  • Time evolution operators: $$ \hat{U}(t, t_0) = \mathcal{T} \exp\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar} \int_{t_0}^t \hat{H}(t') dt'\right) $$ for the full Hamiltonian (time-ordered exponential if $$ \hat{H} $$ is time-dependent), and similarly $$ \hat{U}_0(t, t_0) $$ for $$ \hat{H}_0 $$.
  • Assume $$ t_0 = 0 $$ for simplicity unless specified.

1. The Schrödinger Picture

In the Schrödinger picture, the quantum state evolves over time while operators remain time-independent (unless explicitly time-dependent, like in a time-varying external field). This is the most intuitive formulation for many, as it aligns with wave mechanics where the wave function "spreads" or changes.

Key Features

  • State Vector: $$ \ket{\psi_S(t)} = \hat{U}(t) \ket{\psi_S(0)} $$, where $$ \hat{U}(t) $$ is the unitary time-evolution operator satisfying $$ \hat{U}^\dagger \hat{U} = \hat{I} $$.
  • Operators: $$ \hat{A}_S $$ is static (e.g., position $$ \hat{x} $$ or momentum $$ \hat{p} $$ don't change with time inherently).
  • Expectation Values: $$ \langle \hat{A} \rangle(t) = \bra{\psi_S(t)} \hat{A}_S \ket{\psi_S(t)} $$, which evolves due to the state.

Equation of Motion

The time-dependent Schrödinger equation (TDSE) governs the evolution:

$$ i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \ket{\psi_S(t)} = \hat{H} \ket{\psi_S(t)} $$

For a time-independent $$ \hat{H} $$, the solution is $$ \ket{\psi_S(t)} = e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar} \ket{\psi_S(0)} $$ (using the exponential map).

Derivation Insight

This arises from the postulate that the Hamiltonian generates time translations. In the position basis, it becomes the familiar wave equation for $$ \psi(x,t) $$.

Advantages and Applications

  • Ideal for solving bound-state problems or numerical simulations (e.g., time-dependent density functional theory in chemistry).
  • Example: A free particle. Initial Gaussian wave packet $$ \psi(x,0) $$ spreads over time due to dispersion, calculated via the TDSE.

If $$ \hat{H} $$ is time-dependent, the evolution operator requires the Dyson series for approximation in perturbation theory.

2. The Heisenberg Picture

Here, the state is fixed (time-independent), and operators carry the time dependence. This picture emphasizes observables' evolution, mirroring classical Poisson brackets via commutators.

Key Features

  • State Vector: $$ \ket{\psi_H} = \ket{\psi_S(0)} $$ (frozen at initial time).
  • Operators: $$ \hat{A}_H(t) = \hat{U}^\dagger(t) \hat{A}_S \hat{U}(t) $$.
  • Expectation Values: $$ \langle \hat{A} \rangle(t) = \bra{\psi_H} \hat{A}_H(t) \ket{\psi_H} $$, equivalent to the Schrödinger picture.

Equation of Motion

The Heisenberg equation for any operator $$ \hat{A}_H(t) $$:

$$ \frac{d\hat{A}_H(t)}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar} [\hat{H}, \hat{A}_H(t)] + \frac{\partial \hat{A}_H}{\partial t} $$

(The partial derivative accounts for explicit time dependence in $$ \hat{A} $$.) For time-independent $$ \hat{H} $$ and $$ \hat{A} $$, it simplifies to your provided form.

Derivation from Schrödinger Picture

Start with $$ \langle \hat{A} \rangle(t) = \bra{\psi_S(t)} \hat{A}_S \ket{\psi_S(t)} = \bra{\psi_S(0)} \hat{U}^\dagger(t) \hat{A}_S \hat{U}(t) \ket{\psi_S(0)} $$. Defining $$ \hat{A}_H(t) = \hat{U}^\dagger(t) \hat{A}_S \hat{U}(t) $$ transforms us to the Heisenberg picture. Differentiating yields the equation.

Advantages and Applications

  • Connects to classical mechanics: The commutator $$ [ \cdot, \cdot ] / i\hbar $$ parallels Poisson brackets.
  • Useful in quantum optics or many-body physics for tracking operator correlations.
  • Example: Harmonic oscillator. Position operator $$ \hat{x}_H(t) = \hat{x} \cos(\omega t) + \frac{\hat{p}}{m\omega} \sin(\omega t) $$, showing classical-like oscillation.

This picture is powerful for deriving equations of motion for observables without solving the full state.

3. The Interaction (Dirac) Picture

This hybrid picture is tailored for systems with a dominant free Hamiltonian $$ \hat{H}_0 $$ and a small perturbation $$ \hat{V}(t) $$. It's central to time-dependent perturbation theory, like in quantum field theory or atomic physics.

Key Features

  • State Vector: $$ \ket{\psi_I(t)} = \hat{U}_0^\dagger(t) \ket{\psi_S(t)} $$, evolves slowly under the interaction.
  • Operators: $$ \hat{A}_I(t) = \hat{U}_0^\dagger(t) \hat{A}_S \hat{U}_0(t) $$, evolve under the free Hamiltonian.
  • Interaction Hamiltonian: $$ \hat{V}_I(t) = \hat{U}_0^\dagger(t) \hat{V}(t) \hat{U}_0(t) $$.

Equation of Motion

The interaction-picture TDSE:

$$ i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \ket{\psi_I(t)} = \hat{V}_I(t) \ket{\psi_I(t)} $$

This is slower-evolving than the full TDSE if $$ \hat{V} $$ is weak.

Derivation

From the Schrödinger picture: $$ \ket{\psi_S(t)} = \hat{U}_0(t) \ket{\psi_I(t)} $$. Plug into the TDSE:

$$ i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} [\hat{U}_0(t) \ket{\psi_I(t)}] = \hat{H} \hat{U}_0(t) \ket{\psi_I(t)} $$

Using $$ i\hbar \partial_t \hat{U}_0 = \hat{H}_0 \hat{U}_0 $$, simplify to isolate the $$ \hat{V} $$ term, yielding the above.

For solutions, use the Dyson series:

$$ \ket{\psi_I(t)} = \mathcal{T} \exp\left( -\frac{i}{\hbar} \int_0^t \hat{V}_I(t') dt' \right) \ket{\psi_I(0)} $$

First-order approximation: $$ \ket{\psi_I(t)} \approx \left( \hat{I} - \frac{i}{\hbar} \int_0^t \hat{V}_I(t') dt' \right) \ket{\psi_I(0)} $$, used for transition probabilities.

Advantages and Applications

  • Simplifies perturbation expansions (e.g., Fermi's golden rule for transition rates).
  • Essential in quantum electrodynamics for particle interactions.
  • Example: Two-level atom in a laser field. $$ \hat{H}_0 $$ is the atomic levels, $$ \hat{V}(t) $$ is the dipole interaction. The interaction picture reveals Rabi oscillations.

Relationships and Transformations Between Pictures

All pictures are equivalent—expectation values are identical—but differ in what's time-dependent. Transformations use unitary operators:

  • Schrödinger to Heisenberg: Fix state, evolve operators backward.
  • Schrödinger to Interaction: "Factor out" free evolution from the state.
  • Heisenberg to Interaction: Adjust for $$ \hat{H}_0 $$ vs. full $$ \hat{H} $$.

Mathematically:

  • $$ \ket{\psi_H} = \hat{U}^\dagger(t) \ket{\psi_S(t)} $$ (constant).
  • $$ \ket{\psi_I(t)} = \hat{U}_0^\dagger(t) \hat{U}(t) \ket{\psi_H} $$.

Choose based on the problem: Schrödinger for state-focused simulations, Heisenberg for observables, Interaction for perturbations.
